# GlycoTwin dataset research and selection

**Research snapshot:** 31 October 2026  
**Scope:** Dataset selection only. No model was trained, no source dataset was downloaded, and no application code or existing artifact was changed. Dataset pages, documentation, papers, and public schema previews were inspected online. Where files were access-restricted, this report does not claim direct inspection of their contents.

## Executive recommendation

There is no single dataset that can support all three intended tasks. They have different target definitions, time scales, access rules, and populations.

1. **Model A (cross-sectional diabetes status):** build a better documented, population-based development benchmark from NHANES 2017–2018 or 2021–2023. NHANES combines interview, examination, and lab files with a national sample design; it is not longitudinal onset data. Decide whether the label is *self-reported diagnosed diabetes* or *current diabetes defined by self-report/medication/lab thresholds*. Keep the label definition fixed and remove diagnostic tests from predictors if the goal is pre-diagnostic risk estimation.
2. **Model A (future onset):** keep Pima as a small, historical five-year-onset baseline. It should not be described as equivalent to a current-diagnosis classifier. A larger incident-risk cohort such as UK Biobank would be a stronger future candidate, but new applications are currently paused, with the repository saying it intends to resume in late 2026. NHANES cannot substitute for a true onset cohort.
3. **Model B (glucose forecasting):** begin a reproducible univariate forecast benchmark with the small Hugging Face Gluco TSFM collection only after validating its actual split files and license obligations. It provides participant-level splits and glucose sequences but no insulin, meals, activity, or sleep columns. For a clinically contextualized research cohort, prioritize T1DEXI access through Vivli; for T2D-specific forecasting, evaluate ShanghaiT2DM from Figshare. MetaboNet may offer the broadest consolidated T1D resource, but its current public page and paper give inconsistent counts and the account-gated release/schema must be checked before choosing it as the benchmark.
4. **Model C (profiles and contextual experiments):** use Synthea sample exports for synthetic app profiles and workflow testing only. For real meal–glucose–activity associations, CGMacros is the richest open short study found, but only 45 people and 10 days, with 14 T2D participants; it has no demonstrated sleep or longitudinal clinical history. NHANES is useful for population-level demographic, lab, diet-recall, activity, and selected-cycle sleep analyses, not a time-aligned personal digital twin.

### What can be obtained now

Public-use NHANES XPT files, BRFSS annual files, Pima mirrors, Gluco TSFM Benchmark files, CGMacros, Shanghai datasets, and Synthea samples are reachable without a research application. Check each item’s license/terms before reuse. **CGMacros is 627.9 MB uncompressed** and BIG IDEAs is 34.1 GB uncompressed; neither was downloaded during this investigation. Pima is already present in the ML-service from the earlier implementation. T1DEXI requires a Vivli request, approved research plan/DUA and training; DiaTrend requires Synapse certification and an intended-use statement; T1DiabetesGranada requires an authenticated Zenodo request and its DUA; OhioT1DM asks for an institutional-email request and says processing may take a week; MetaboNet requires an account and has tiered access. These are not immediate anonymous downloads.

## Task A — Diabetes risk classification

### Task distinction and target design

The word “risk” is ambiguous. A classifier predicting *already present diabetes* is a prevalence/diagnosis classifier. Predicting *future onset* requires a documented baseline and a future outcome window, with people disease-free at baseline. A complications model needs a complication-specific endpoint and time origin. These outcomes cannot be pooled or scored as if they were the same task.

The Pima database is often called a diagnosis dataset in modern tutorials, but its historical source is Smith et al., “Using the ADAP learning algorithm to forecast the onset of diabetes mellitus.” The curated UCI/OpenML description and documentation based on that paper describe the label as development of diabetes during a five-year follow-up. Treat it as a historical onset task unless the exact downloaded version’s provenance disproves that definition. Features are the baseline pregnancy count, 2-hour OGTT glucose, diastolic blood pressure, triceps skinfold, 2-hour insulin, BMI, pedigree score, and age. Its 2-hour glucose and insulin are costly clinical measurements, and a 5-year outcome in a narrow cohort is not a modern general screening target.

Sources: [original Pima provenance and schema in the UCI/OpenML archive](https://github.com/datasets/openml-datasets/blob/main/data/diabetes/README.md), [Smith et al. paper record](https://doi.org/10.1109/SCAMC.1988.7700), and [Pima source documentation](https://search.r-project.org/CRAN/refmans/LSTbook/html/PIDD.html).

For NHANES, define the label explicitly:

- **Self-reported diagnosed status:** interview variable `DIQ010` (cycle suffix varies) captures whether a health professional told the participant they have diabetes; it misses undiagnosed cases and is a prevalence label.
- **Lab-defined current status:** HbA1c (`LBXGH`) and, for a fasting subsample, plasma glucose (`LBXGLU`) can identify undiagnosed hyperglycemia. Choose and cite a clinical definition in the analysis protocol. Do not treat a self-report-only label and a lab composite as interchangeable. Apply fasting/subsample weights correctly.
- **Future onset:** NHANES public-use cycles are cross-sectional samples, not repeated observations of the same participant. Some external linkages exist under separate NCHS rules, but the ordinary public participant files do not give a cohort followed for new-onset diabetes. Do not call its classification results incidence prediction.

For the inspected August 2021–August 2023 release, NCHS reports 11,933 interview respondents and 8,860 MEC-examined participants. `DIQ010` distinguishes yes/no/borderline and `DIQ_L` also contains current insulin/pill variables; `DEMO_L` supplies `SEQN`, `RIAGENDR`, `RIDAGEYR`, `RIDRETH3`, cycle and sample weights. `BMXBMI` has 389 missing values among 8,860 records; `LBXGH` has 484 missing among 7,199 lab records; the fasting glucose file is a fasting subsample. These are module counts, not the final eligible diabetes-model sample. See [DIQ_L](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/DIQ_L.htm), [DEMO_L](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/DEMO_L.htm), [BMX_L](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/BMX_L.htm), [GHB_L](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/Nchs/Data/Nhanes/Public/2021/DataFiles/GHB_L.htm), and [NCHS cycle response rates](https://wwwn.cdc.gov/nchs/data/ResponseRates/NHANES-August-2021-August-2023-Response-Rates.pdf).

## Task B — Glucose forecasting

### Forecast horizons and split design

At five-minute cadence, 30, 60 and 120 minutes correspond to 6, 12 and 24 steps ahead; at 15-minute cadence, they correspond to 2, 4 and 8 steps. Use only timestamps and valid observed glucose values for target creation; a horizon exists only where the future measurement is observed. Report gaps, not just the nominal cadence. Do not fill across long gaps or let interpolation use future points across the prediction cutoff.

Use patient-level train/validation/test partitions for population generalization. For personalized forecasting, train on an earlier contiguous block for each held-out person and test on a later block, with a separate unseen-person test. Keep overlapping windows from one person and neighboring timestamps within one partition. Fit scaling/imputation on training history only. Report subject-macro and pooled metrics; pooled samples otherwise let long recordings dominate.

## Critical methodology and validity controls

### Model A

- Pre-register endpoint, time zero, outcome window, age exclusions, diagnostic threshold, and treatment of borderline/gestational diabetes. Separate prevalent diagnosis, five-year onset and complication endpoints.
- Enforce prediction-time availability. For a pre-diagnosis risk score, do not include post-diagnosis medication, diagnostic labs that define the label, or downstream care. For a diagnosis classifier, clearly call it detection/classification and disclose that glucose/HbA1c may be part of the clinical definition.
- Deduplicate by participant, not row; put all records for a person in one group. For population surveys, account for strata/PSUs/weights and cycle-specific weights; ordinary random stratification does not preserve the sampling design.
- Use nested or training-only validation, then untouched test, then external/out-of-time validation with the same target definition. Report ROC-AUC, PR-AUC, sensitivity/recall, specificity, precision, F1 at prespecified thresholds, confusion matrices, Brier score, calibration curve/slope/intercept and confidence intervals.
- Class imbalance: preserve natural test prevalence, avoid accuracy-only reporting and naive oversampling; tune operating point based on an explicit use case, not on test data. Recalibrate only on an allowed calibration sample.
- Test shifts across age/sex/race/ethnicity/socioeconomic strata and cycles. Avoid causal claims from importance or observational predictors. Quantify uncertain and missing-data handling.

### Model B

- Split by participant for population transfer and by future time block within person for personalization. If using overlapping input/target windows, purge or embargo near-boundary windows. Never random-split CGM rows.
- Construct targets only at valid measured timestamps; report gap rate and horizon coverage. Fit scalers/imputers on training data only. Preserve timezone and device/units metadata. Check for event logging and lagging/clock drift before joining meals, insulin and activity.
- Avoid future leakage from interpolation, whole-series normalization, outcome-derived windows, post-event summaries, or labels computed using readings after the forecast cutoff. Specifically inspect benchmark preprocessing and interpolation artifacts.
- Report horizon-specific MAE/RMSE and patient-macro metrics; MARD with caveats; clinical error-grid analyses only as secondary descriptions. Report hypoglycemic/hyperglycemic range errors, calibration of prediction intervals and per-person variation.
- Distinguish transfer to new people, personalized within-person forecasting, and forecasting with known future covariates (planned meals/insulin) as separate evaluations. T1D insulin-use datasets do not establish T2D performance.

### Model C

- Keep simulated EHR resources visibly synthetic. Use them to verify app workflows, not estimate clinical distributions or validate prediction.
- NHANES modules can be joined by cycle and `SEQN`, but weights/eligibility differ by component. Self-report and lab fields can disagree by design.
- Do not claim meal, sleep and activity “cause” glucose changes from small observational cohorts. Confounding, adherence, sensor measurement error, intervention design and selection into CGM use all matter.
- No examined source justifies a physiological digital twin that simulates interventions or individual treatment response. Such a claim requires prospective patient-level data, validated mechanisms, consent/governance, and external clinical evaluation.
